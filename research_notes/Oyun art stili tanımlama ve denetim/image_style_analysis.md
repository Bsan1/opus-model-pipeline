# Quantitative art-style analysis from reference images (input for an automated 3D asset pipeline)

Scope: measurable style descriptors computable in Python on a Windows PC (numpy, Pillow, scikit-image, OpenCV; optional torch), what each tells an artist, cost, and how each breaks on perspective game frames (UI, lighting, compression). Context: the repo already has `tools/Style.py` (sRGB to Lab, k-means palette, chroma, gamut); this catalogue covers what such a tool could add.

Convention in this file: "Cited Findings" are from sources. "Inferences" hold my engineering recipes and judgement (parameters marked "suggested" are mine, not from a source; compute times marked "estimate" were not measured).

---

## 1. Colour: palette, harmony, hue families, saturation/chroma, temperature, shadow/light shift, contrast

### Takeaway
Work in a perceptual space (OKLab/OKLCh): pixel k-means or median-cut gives the palette, a chroma-weighted hue histogram gives hue families, and Cohen-Or's rotatable hue templates give a harmony type plus a fit score. Report warm/cool as an OKLab b mean (and CCT only for near-neutral lit areas), and measure the shadow vs light shift as a signed Δa/Δb/ΔL between dark and lit pixels of the same region. All of these are cheap (numpy-level, well under a second per downscaled image).

### Cited Findings
- **OKLab** is a perceptual Lab-type space (L lightness, a green-red, b blue-yellow), D65 white like sRGB. In its author's hue-prediction test the RMS hue error is 0.49 vs 0.69 for CIELAB (95th percentile 1.06 vs 1.56). CIELAB shifts hue toward purple when white is blended with blue, and OKLab avoids that. The conversion is linear sRGB to LMS by a 3x3 matrix, cube root, then a second 3x3 matrix. OKLCh: C = sqrt(a²+b²), h = atan2(b,a). The reference code is public domain, with an MIT option. — [Ottosson, "A perceptual color space for image processing"](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/)
- **Hue harmony templates (Cohen-Or et al. 2006)**: 8 template types (i, V, L, I, T, Y, X, N), taken from Matsuda's schemes and defined as sectors on the HSV hue wheel. Each can be rotated by any angle α. N stands for grayscale images and is not used for fitting. Sector sizes from the paper's appendix: the large sectors of V, Y and X are 26% of the disk (93.6°); the small sectors of i, L, I and Y are 5% (18°); the large sector of L is 22% (79.2°); T is 50% (180°). The two sector centres of I, X and Y are 180° apart, and those of L are 90° apart. — [Cohen-Or et al., Color Harmonization, SIGGRAPH 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/07/harmonization.pdf)
- **Harmony fit function**: F(X,(m,α)) = Σ_p ‖H(p) − E_Tm(α)(p)‖ · S(p), where ‖·‖ is the arc-length distance on the hue wheel in radians to the nearest sector border, hues inside a sector count as distance 0, and each pixel is weighted by its saturation S because hue differences of low-saturation colours are less noticeable. The best α for each template is found by minimisation (Brent's method), and the best template is the one with the lowest F. — [Cohen-Or et al. 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2006/07/harmonization.pdf)
- A later palette-based method (Tan et al.) replaces the sector templates with axis-based templates derived from classical colour theory and works on the extracted palette instead of per-pixel hues. — [Palette-based image decomposition, harmonization, and color transfer (arXiv 1804.01225)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.01225)
- **Colourfulness (Hasler & Süsstrunk 2003, "M3")**: rg = R − G, yb = 0.5(R+G) − B; M = sqrt(σ_rg² + σ_yb²) + 0.3·sqrt(μ_rg² + μ_yb²). — [ColorNet paper (arXiv 1908.08505)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.08505); [pyaesthetics colourfulness docs](https://prettywebsite.readthedocs.io/en/stable/colorfulness.html). One implementation maps values to labels from the original rating experiment: 0 not colourful, 33 moderately, 59 quite, 109 extremely colourful. — [HSM3 (R package) README](https://github.com/DrNickRedfern/HSM3)
- **Colour entropy**: the Aesthetics Toolbox measures colourfulness as the Shannon entropy of the HSV hue channel. It is high when many hues appear about equally often and very low for a single-hue image. It also reports means and standard deviations of every channel in RGB, HSV and CIELAB, and warns that the choice of colour model (even sRGB vs Adobe RGB) can strongly change the computed values. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper (arXiv 2408.10616)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **RMS contrast** = standard deviation of CIELAB L* (Peli 1990). Several studies find high-contrast images preferred. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **Colour temperature**: `colour.xy_to_CCT` (colour-science) converts CIE xy chromaticity to correlated colour temperature with selectable methods. v0.3.x offered 'McCamy 1992' (the default) and 'Hernandez 1999'; the latest docs default to 'CIE Illuminant D Series' and add 'Kang 2002'. For xy (0.3127, 0.3290), McCamy gives about 6508 K. — [colour-science xy_to_CCT docs (latest)](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/colour.xy_to_CCT.html); [v0.3.12 docs](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/v0.3.12/generated/colour.xy_to_CCT.html). The Hernández-Andrés 1999 method uses two epicentres and covers roughly 3,000 K to 10⁶ K. — [colour-science Hernandez1999 docs](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/v0.4.3/generated/colour.temperature.xy_to_CCT_Hernandez1999.html)
- **Palette extraction libraries**: Pylette offers k-means (default) or median-cut quantisation, resizing before extraction for speed, alpha-channel transparency masking, a CLI, CSV export and weighted colour sampling. — [Pylette on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pylette/). Color Thief uses Modified Median Cut Quantization (MMCQ). fast_colorthief (C++ backend) reports about 0.034 s per 1200x1200 JPEG vs about 0.509 s for the pure-Python reference. — [fast-colorthief on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/fast-colorthief/). Median cut (Heckbert 1982) splits colour space into boxes with roughly equal pixel counts. — [Leptonica colorquant2 docs](https://www.metacpan.org/pod/Image::Leptonica::Func::colorquant2)

### Inferences
Recipes (suggested parameters):
- **Pre-processing**: decode to float, sRGB to linear to OKLab; downscale the long side to about 512 px for colour statistics (colour means and standard deviations depend little on size; see section 8); apply the UI/HUD mask (section 8).
- **Palette (k = 5-12)**: k-means on OKLab pixels (sample about 50k pixels; `sklearn.cluster.KMeans` or the repo's own `kmeans`). Report each cluster's OKLCh centre, area share and spatial spread. A *weighted* palette (by area) answers "what colours dominate"; a separate *accent* palette (clusters with C above the 90th percentile but area under 5%) answers "what are the pop colours". Median cut (Pylette/colorthief) is faster but splits along RGB box axes, so it is less perceptual. Use it as a cross-check only. To compare palettes between reference and render, a ΔE_OK-based Earth Mover's Distance between weighted palettes is more robust than matching colours one to one.
- **Hue families**: a 36-bin (10°) OKLCh hue histogram weighted by chroma (so greys do not vote, the same idea as Cohen-Or's saturation weighting). Find its peaks and report the number of hue families, their centres and widths, and the share of near-neutral pixels (C below about 0.02-0.03 in OKLab units). For an artist this answers "limited palette vs rainbow" and "which hue carries the scene".
- **Harmony template**: compute the Cohen-Or F for all 7 templates with a brute-force α sweep in 1° steps over a 360-bin chroma-weighted histogram. That is about 7 x 360 x 360 operations, which is negligible. Report the best template, α, and F normalised by total weight (radians per unit weight), plus the runner-up. Using OKLCh hue and chroma instead of HSV is my substitution, not the paper's: HSV hue is not perceptually uniform, so template widths fixed in degrees mean different things in each space. Keep the space consistent between reference and render.
- **Saturation/chroma distribution**: OKLCh C percentiles (p10/p50/p90), chroma vs lightness joint histogram (stylised games often keep high C in midtones and drop C in darks and lights), Hasler-Süsstrunk M3 as a familiar single number.
- **Warm/cool**: mean and median OKLab b (positive = yellow/warm, negative = blue/cool) and a, computed separately for lit, midtone and shadow value bands. Also compute CCT (colour-science) from the mean linear RGB of the top-lightness, low-chroma pixels only, i.e. the white-ish lit surfaces, which approximate the light colour. CCT is not meaningful for saturated stylised scenes because chromaticities far from the Planckian locus give a correlated temperature that means little.
- **Shadow vs light colour shift** (the "shadows go purple/blue" rule): within each segmented region (section 4) or k-means colour cluster of similar albedo, split pixels into shadow (OKLab L below the region's 25th percentile) and lit (above the 75th). Report ΔL, Δa, Δb, ΔC and the circular hue difference Δh (shadow minus lit), then aggregate by area-weighted median. Δb < 0 means shadows are cooler (bluer). Δa > 0 with Δb < 0 means a purple-ish shift. ΔC < 0 means shadows are desaturated. Comparing *within* a region avoids confusing different materials with shading. Better still, after intrinsic decomposition (section 5) measure the colour of the shading layer directly.
- **Contrast**: global RMS contrast (std of L), Michelson-type p95/p5 lightness range, plus *chromatic contrast* as the std of a and b. Separate "value contrast" from "colour contrast" because stylised art often has low value contrast but high hue contrast.
- **What it tells the artist**: palette swatches plus shares, hue families, harmony type ("analogous V-type around orange, complementary accent"), chroma ceilings by value band, warm-light/cool-shadow amounts in measurable ΔE terms.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable primary source quantifying how much shadow hue shifts in painted or stylised game art. The warm-light/cool-shadow rule attributed to James Gurney appeared in my search only via low-reliability copies of his book, so I do not cite it. Thresholds for "noticeable shift" would need calibration on the user's own reference sets.
- No source validated the Cohen-Or templates in OKLab/OKLCh. The original uses HSV.
- Not verified: CCT accuracy ranges of the McCamy method (only the Hernández range was confirmed).

## 2. Value: histograms, notan / value grouping, global and local contrast, focal points

### Takeaway
Value structure is measured on a lightness channel (OKLab L or CIELAB L*): percentiles and a histogram give key (high/low) and range, a 3- or 5-level posterisation gives the notan, and its area shares plus fragmentation (connected components) quantify value grouping. RMS contrast is the standard global measure. Local contrast and spectral-residual saliency locate focal points. All are classical and run in milliseconds to tens of milliseconds on a downscaled image (estimate).

### Cited Findings
- RMS contrast = std of the CIELAB L* channel; lightness entropy = Shannon entropy of pixel intensities (high when all values occur about equally often). The Toolbox also has Homogeneity, the relative entropy of the *spatial* distribution of intensity (horizontal and vertical averaged), which unlike lightness entropy is low when intensity is concentrated in one region. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- A three-value notan uses black, white and one mid grey for the main dark and light masses. A practical method is to posterize the reference to 3 levels. — [Virtual Art Academy: Notan](https://www.virtualartacademy.com/notan/); [Draw Paint Academy: Notan](https://drawpaintacademy.com/notan/)
- Strong designs group lights together and darks together, as opposed to scattering values. Whistler's "Mother" is cited as linking the darks into one large shape. The third value works as an accent. — [Draw Paint Academy: Notan](https://drawpaintacademy.com/notan/); [Artists Network: The value of notan](https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-mediums/pastel/the-value-of-notan/)
- Balance and symmetry measures (pixel-based mirror symmetry, Wilson & Chatterjee balance score, Deviation of the Center of Mass, DCM) are expressed as percentages. In a rating study, DCM correlated more strongly with subjective balance than the balance score. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **Saliency**: OpenCV contrib's `StaticSaliencySpectralResidual` follows Hou & Zhang (CVPR 2007), deriving saliency from the residual of the log spectrum. `StaticSaliencyFineGrained` uses centre-surround differences. Both are created with `cv2.saliency.*_create()` and run `computeSaliency`. — [OpenCV saliency module docs](https://docs.opencv.org/4.5.4/dc/d0d/namespacecv_1_1saliency.html); [Hou & Zhang 2007](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2007/hou2007cvpr-saliency); [PyImageSearch tutorial](https://www.pyimagesearch.com/2018/07/16/opencv-saliency-detection/). One user reports OpenCV's spectral residual output does not match the paper's reference implementation. — [OpenCV Q&A](https://answers.opencv.org/questions/67279/revisions)

### Inferences
Recipes (suggested parameters):
- **Value histogram**: 32-bin histogram of OKLab L (0-1). Report p5/p25/p50/p75/p95, mean and skew. "Key" = median L (high-key above about 0.65, low-key below about 0.35 are suggested, uncalibrated cut-offs). Range = p95 − p5. Bimodality (for example Sarle's coefficient) separates two-value designs from smooth tonal gradation.
- **Notan**: `skimage.filters.threshold_multiotsu(L, classes=3)` and `classes=5` (or 1-D k-means on L). Report:
  1. area share per value group (for example 20/55/25%),
  2. the centre L of each group, so the gaps between groups measure how clearly the values separate,
  3. fragmentation = the number of connected components per group above 0.1% of the area, plus the share of the largest component in each group. Low fragmentation means good grouping.
  4. the variance of L *within* each group: low variance means flat, graphic value design.

  Save the notan image as a board thumbnail. It is the most artist-readable output.
- **Local contrast**: std of L in a Gaussian window (σ about 1.5-3% of image width), averaged and as a map. The ratio local/global contrast shows whether detail contrast is subdued inside big shapes (typical of painterly and stylised work) or as strong as the global contrast (typical of photos and busy realism). A band-limited contrast pyramid (Peli-style, Laplacian pyramid levels) gives contrast per spatial scale, which complements section 3.
- **Focal points**: combine (a) spectral-residual saliency, (b) a local-contrast peak and (c) a chroma peak. Report the top 1-3 peak locations as normalised (x,y), the peak-to-mean saliency ratio (a single dominant focal point vs evenly busy) and whether the max-contrast and max-chroma regions coincide. A rule-of-thirds or centre distance is optional. In games, the player character or crosshair is often at the centre by camera design, so treat centre bias as a camera property, not as style.
- **What it tells the artist**: "3 values with 20/55/25 area, darks grouped into 2 large masses, little contrast inside shapes, focal accent where the highest chroma meets the strongest value contrast".

### Gaps
- No source gives quantitative norms for notan area ratios or fragmentation in game art. A 60/25/15 value-proportion tip appeared in a search snippet from art-tutorial sites, but I could not confirm which site or author it came from.
- No source validated spectral-residual saliency on stylised game frames. The OpenCV implementation itself has a reported discrepancy.

## 3. Detail and edges: edge density, sharpness, spectral slope, busyness, noise; painterly vs realistic

### Takeaway
The best-documented "detail" descriptors come from empirical aesthetics. These are the Fourier amplitude-spectrum slope (natural scenes about 1.2; steeper = fewer fine details), gradient-based complexity, Gabor edge density, edge-orientation entropy, PHOG self-similarity and anisotropy. All exist in Python in the open-source Aesthetics Toolbox. Add a blur/sharpness metric (scikit-image `blur_effect`) and a noise estimate. Every one of these is size- and implementation-dependent, so resize all images to one fixed size and use one implementation.

### Cited Findings
- **Fourier slope**: transform the grayscale image, radially average the spectrum, fit a line in log-log, and take the negative slope. Amplitude slopes of natural scenes average 0.8-1.5 (peak about 1.2). The power-spectrum slope is about twice that (peak about 2.4). Shallower slopes (less negative) mean more fine detail, so the slope also works as a complexity measure. Traditional artworks have slopes similar to natural scenes. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- Implementation details change absolute values and even between-group correlations. Three bundled variants are: Spehar (8-bit grey, centre crop to the largest power-of-2 square, amplitude, drop points with Cook's distance > n/4), Redies (pad to square with mean grey, resize to 1024x1024, power spectrum, fit only 10-256 cycles/image, binned in log-log), and Mather (CIELAB L, centre crop, resize to 1024, amplitude, drop the lowest and highest frequency quartiles). Frequencies are excluded because of artefacts such as uneven illumination, rectangular sampling, raster screens and noise. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- Art images and natural scenes share roughly 1/f² power fall-off, and Koch et al. (2010) found similar properties in cartoons, comics and graphic novels. — [Koch, Denzler & Redies 2010, PLoS ONE](https://doaj.org/article/3b3446d3895042209de57c074ce8b206); [Redies et al. 2007, Fractal-like image statistics in visual art](https://pub.inf-cv.uni-jena.de/pdf/Redies07:FIS). Photographs of faces have steeper slopes, while artists' portraits have the scale-invariant slopes of complex natural scenes. — [Redies et al. 2007, Artists portray human faces with the Fourier statistics of complex natural scenes](https://pub.inf-cv.uni-jena.de/pdf/Redies07:Aph). Artworks and graphic novels are more homogeneous (isotropic) across Fourier orientations than photographs of objects or scenes. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper, citing Koch et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **Fractal dimension** D (box counting) is inversely related to the Fourier slope. Perceived complexity increases linearly with D across natural images, paintings and synthetic patterns. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **Complexity (HOG method)**: convert to CIELAB, take the gradient magnitude per channel and the max across channels (L* dominates), then the mean of the combined gradient image. The value depends on image size, so use a uniform input size. **Edge density (Gabor)**: grayscale, reduce to at most 120,000 pixels, apply 24 oriented Gabor filters over 360°, and sum all responses. It reflects both edge count and strength and correlates with perceived complexity. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **PHOG self-similarity**: normalise size (for example to 100,000 px total), compute Lab gradients, HOG with 8 bins/180° or 16 bins/360°, pyramid levels 1-3 (2x2, 4x4, 8x8), and compare each level with level 0 by histogram intersection. Values near 1 mean high self-similarity. Traditional paintings sit intermediate to high. Above level 3 the values become unstable. **Anisotropy**: the std of normalised gradient strength across orientation bins in the 64 level-3 subregions. It is high when specific orientations dominate (for example the horizontals and verticals of building facades), and traditional Western artworks tend to be less anisotropic than non-art images. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **Edge-orientation entropy (EOE)**: first order = how uniform the edge-orientation histogram is; second order = how independent edge orientations are across positions. Artworks from many cultures show high EOE, shared with natural growth patterns but not with many photos of man-made scenes. — [Redies et al. 2017, Frontiers in Neuroscience (PMC5660963)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5660963/); [Grebenkina et al. 2018 (PMC6172329)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6172329/). Second-order EOE is by far the most expensive measure (it compares each of the 10,000 strongest edges with all others), and a Cython/C++ version gives more than 100x speed-up. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616); [C++ 2nd-order EOE repo](https://github.com/RBartho/C-version-2nd-Order-Edge-Orientation-Entropy)
- **Blur / sharpness**: `skimage.measure.blur_effect(image, h_size=11, channel_axis=None, reduce_func=np.max)` returns 0 (sharp) to 1 (maximally blurred), converting colour to grey first. h_size must be the same across compared images, and 11 correlates best with human perception. It is based on Crété-Roffet et al. 2007 (re-blur and compare neighbouring-pixel variations). — [scikit-image blur_effect example](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/_sources/auto_examples/filters/plot_blur_effect.rst.txt); [Crété et al. 2007 (HAL)](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00232709)
- **Edge-width blur (Marziliano et al. 2002)**: the average edge spread over detected vertical Sobel edges. Its refinement is just-noticeable-blur (JNB, Narvekar & Karam). Edge-width metrics are sensitive to the edge-threshold choice and to noise. — [arXiv 1810.08169 (review section)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08169)

### Inferences
Recipes (suggested parameters):
- **Fixed analysis size**: resize every image to 1024 px on the long side with one resampler (for example Pillow LANCZOS) before any detail metric. Never compare values computed at different sizes or with different libraries (section 8).
- **Fourier slope**: use the Redies variant (pad to a square with mean grey, 1024², power spectrum, fit 10-256 cycles/image). The upper cut-off helps against JPEG/video block artefacts. Also report the *orientation profile*: spectral energy in 8-16 orientation wedges, giving its isotropy and peak count. Sharp peaks at 0°/90° mean architectural or hard-surface content or a strong HUD frame. Cost: one 1024² FFT is well under a second in numpy (estimate).
- **Busyness**: combine HOG complexity, Gabor edge density and the Canny edge-pixel fraction (`cv2.Canny` on a slightly blurred L channel, thresholds from median-based auto thresholds) into three numbers. Compute them per region as well as globally: stylised art often concentrates detail at focal points and keeps large shapes quiet, so the *ratio of edge density in the salient region to the rest* is a good style descriptor.
- **Edge softness profile**: (a) `blur_effect` (global), (b) a histogram of 10-90% edge rise widths in pixels, measured along gradient normals at Canny edges (a Marziliano-style measure but using all orientations), (c) the share of "lost" edges (low gradient across a value-group boundary from section 2). Hard cel/toon edges give rise widths of about 1-2 px; painterly work gives a broad distribution (inference).
- **Noise / grain**: `skimage.restoration.estimate_sigma` (wavelet-based) on flat regions (low local gradient). Report it separately so film grain or dithering is not counted as "detail".
- **Texture density inside materials**: the std of L inside large colour segments after removing low-frequency shading (subtract a Gaussian blur with σ about 2% of width). Flat-coloured "stylised" surfaces score near zero, painted-texture surfaces are moderate, and photoreal surfaces are high. This is the descriptor closest to the repo's "not plastic" rule.
- **Stylised vs realistic signature (hypothesis to calibrate)**: steeper slope and lower edge density in big shapes, low within-material texture std, higher EOE and lower anisotropy for organic painterly scenes. Hard-surface low-poly scenes would show high anisotropy and high orientation-spectrum peaks with low texture std. Cartoons and comics also sit near 1/f², so slope alone does not separate cartoon from photo (see the Koch finding). Use the multi-feature profile.

### Gaps
- I found no published slope or EOE norms for *3D game renders* or low-poly/toon styles. All norms are for paintings, photos and comics.
- No direct comparison of `blur_effect` vs Laplacian-variance vs edge-width on stylised CG was found.

## 4. Shape language: silhouettes, roundness vs angularity, proportions, big/medium/small rhythm

### Takeaway
Silhouette metrics (circularity, solidity, polygon-vertex count, turning-angle/curvature histograms) are standard OpenCV contour operations once you have masks. Getting clean masks from game frames is the hard part. MobileSAM gives class-agnostic masks at about 10 ms/image on GPU and seconds on CPU. Orientation statistics (edge-orientation entropy, Fourier orientation peaks) give a mask-free angular-vs-curved signal backed by literature.

### Cited Findings
- Curved stimuli have a more homogeneous edge-orientation histogram than angular ones and are generally preferred. Edge-orientation distributions therefore carry information on how angular or curved stimuli are. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616); [Grebenkina et al. 2018 (PMC6172329)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6172329/)
- Curvilinear stimuli show fewer peaks in orientation-summed Fourier magnitude plots than angular ones (Watier 2024), so the Fourier spectrum gives objective angular-vs-curvilinear cues. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- **MobileSAM**: about 10 ms per image on a single GPU (8 ms encoder + 4 ms decoder), about 5x faster and 7x smaller than FastSAM, and it "can run relatively smoothly on CPU". — [MobileSAM paper (arXiv 2306.14289)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289v2). An Ultralytics docs mirror gives about 3 s per image on a Mac i5 CPU (anecdotal, not a benchmark). — [Ultralytics docs mirror](https://strade.fit.vutbr.cz/git/xdobro23/YOLOv8-16bit/commit/201e69e4e4d1fe9fafe4214c4e90bef514d8aea9). Kornia also packages MobileSAM. — [Kornia MobileSAM docs](https://kornia.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models/mobile_sam.html)
- Aspect ratio in the Toolbox is width/height. Changing the aspect ratio for analysis distorts depicted objects and should be avoided. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)

### Inferences
Recipes (suggested parameters):
- **Masks**: (1) Preferred: user-supplied or quick manual masks for key assets in concept art and screenshots. (2) MobileSAM "everything" mode on a 1024 px image, keeping masks above 0.5% of the area. (3) Classical fallback without torch: `skimage.segmentation.felzenszwalb` / SLIC on OKLab, or GrabCut with a user box. For a turntable render of the produced asset, the alpha channel is the exact mask, which is an advantage on the render side.
- **Per-silhouette metrics** (OpenCV `findContours` on the mask):
  - circularity 4πA/P²: perimeter depends on pixel noise, so smooth the contour first (for example resample to 256 points);
  - solidity A/A_convex-hull;
  - convexity-defect count and depth;
  - polygon vertex count from `cv2.approxPolyDP` at ε = 1% and 3% of the perimeter: few vertices at 3% means simple, angular big shapes;
  - turning-angle histogram along the resampled contour: the share of the length in near-straight runs vs share in smooth curves vs share in sharp corners (> 45° within a few samples) gives a *roundness vs angularity* ratio;
  - min-area-rect aspect ratio and principal-axis tilt.
- **Mask-free angularity**: EOE (1st order) and the Fourier-orientation peak count from section 3, measured on the L channel. These work on whole frames, but the effect of architecture and HUD lines must be removed by masking.
- **Shape rhythm (big/medium/small)**: take segment areas as a fraction of the frame and histogram their log10 area. Report the share of the frame covered by the top 1, top 3 and top 10 segments, and the slope of the rank-size distribution. Designs with deliberate "big-medium-small" hierarchy show a steep fall-off (one or two dominant shapes) rather than many similar sizes. Compare reference and render at the *same framing*.
- **Proportions**: for characters, pose keypoints (for example MediaPipe/OpenPose) give a head-to-body ratio and limb thickness ratios. For props and buildings, bounding-box ratios of parts from masks. Perspective changes 2-D ratios, so measure on near-orthographic views (concept art turnarounds, side views) or correct with known vanishing lines.
- **What it tells the artist**: "silhouettes mostly convex (solidity 0.9), corners rounded, few vertices: chunky friendly shapes"; "one big mass covers 40% of the frame, then small detail clusters".

### Gaps
- No source benchmarked SAM-family masks on stylised game frames or concept art. Mask quality on painterly art is unverified.
- I found no literature thresholds for "round" vs "angular" contour metrics in art. The turning-angle recipe needs calibration.
- Proportion analysis for characters (keypoint models on stylised, non-human proportions) was not researched.

## 5. Lighting cues: key/fill ratio, ambient occlusion, rim light, specular highlights, atmospheric perspective

### Takeaway
Lighting can only be estimated heuristically from single images. Practical proxies are: the lit/shadow lightness ratio within same-albedo regions (key/fill, in stops), a darkening profile at contacts and creases (AO), a bright boundary band inside silhouettes (rim), the share of tiny near-white low-chroma blobs (specular), and dark-channel/contrast/saturation trends with depth or image height (haze). Learned intrinsic decomposition (shading vs albedo) makes these cleaner, but it is a heavier torch model.

### Cited Findings
- **Dark channel prior (He, Sun, Tang, CVPR 2009 / TPAMI 2011)**: in most non-sky local patches some pixels have very low intensity in at least one RGB channel. In haze, airlight dominates those dark pixels, so the dark channel directly estimates haze transmission, and a depth map comes out as a by-product (refined by soft matting). — [He et al., TPAMI paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/pami10dehaze.pdf)
- **Intrinsic decomposition**: Careaga & Aksoy (ACM TOG 2023) estimate ordinal shading at low and high resolution and fuse them into albedo and shading, trained with multi-illumination data so it generalises to in-the-wild images. — [Intrinsic Image Decomposition via Ordinal Shading (arXiv 2311.12792)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12792v1). The follow-up "Colorful Diffuse Intrinsic Image Decomposition in the Wild" (2024) handles coloured shading. — [arXiv 2409.13690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13690v1)

### Inferences
Recipes (suggested parameters; all are heuristics, not validated):
- **Key/fill ratio**: inside each segment (or colour cluster of similar albedo), take linear luminance Y at p90 (lit) and p10 (shadow), and report log2(Y90/Y10) in stops, aggregated as the area-weighted median. Flat cel-shaded styles show about 1 stop or a bimodal two-tone; dramatic lighting shows 3 stops or more. Toon shading also shows *bimodality*: few pixels at intermediate luminance within a segment, so report the "terminator softness" = the share of the segment's pixels between its two value modes.
- **Shading vs albedo**: if torch is acceptable, run an intrinsic decomposition (above) and compute the key/fill ratio, shadow colour (section 1) and AO on the *shading* layer. This removes texture/albedo variation from lighting statistics.
- **Ambient occlusion strength**: (a) at object-ground contact lines (bottom edge of segments), the luminance drop in a band 1-3% of the object height vs the ground 5-10% away; (b) in concavities (contour convexity defects, crease edges) vs nearby flat areas. Report the median drop in stops.
- **Rim light**: for each silhouette, mean L in an inner band 1-2% of the object size along the boundary vs the interior median L. A positive gap on one side (often top or back-lit side), with high L and moderate chroma, signals rim light. Report the share of the silhouette length with a rim.
- **Specular highlights**: connected blobs with L above p99, C low (near-white) or matching the light colour, area under 0.05% each. Report blob count per megapixel and the share of segments containing any. Stylised hand-painted styles often paint specular shapes as larger flat shapes, so record the size distribution too.
- **Atmospheric perspective / haze**: the dark channel (minimum over RGB in a 15x15 patch, as in He et al.) plotted against image row (as a proxy for depth in horizon-up game cameras), plus local RMS contrast and mean chroma vs row. A positive slope of the dark channel with distance, and falling contrast and chroma, means haze. The colour of the haze (airlight) is the mean colour of the brightest 0.1% dark-channel pixels. For stylised fog, report its colour in OKLCh (often a tinted, not grey, fog).
- **Light direction (optional)**: the dominant gradient direction of the shading layer on large convex objects gives a 2-D light direction. Consistency across objects separates single-key-light scenes from flat ambient.

### Gaps
- I found no source that validates single-image key/fill ratio, AO strength or rim-light estimation for stylised art. These are my proposed heuristics.
- The compute cost and Windows install effort of the Careaga & Aksoy intrinsic models were not checked (no pip package was confirmed).
- The dark channel prior assumes natural outdoor statistics. Its behaviour on flat-shaded stylised scenes (large saturated flat areas with no dark pixels) is unverified and likely biased toward "hazy".

## 6. Learned style metrics: Gram matrices, CLIP / DINO(v2), CSD, aesthetic scores, LPIPS / DreamSim; reliability and local cost

### Takeaway
For "is this render in the same style as these references", the best published style-retrieval embedding is CSD (CLIP-initialised ViT fine-tuned for style). It beats CLIP, DINO, MoCo, SSCD and VGG Gram matrices on WikiArt and DomainNet, but all generic embeddings remain partly content-biased. LPIPS and DreamSim measure perceptual similarity of an image *pair* (layout and content), not shared style, and aesthetic predictors score "likeability", not style match. Use learned metrics only as a secondary, relative signal: rank a render against target-style and off-style references with similar content, never as an absolute pass/fail.

### Cited Findings
- **CSD results (zero-shot mAP@1)** — DomainNet / WikiArt: CSD ViT-L 78.3 / 64.56; CSD ViT-B 78.3 / 56.2; CLIP ViT-L 74.0 / 59.4; CLIP ViT-B/16 73.7 / 52.2; DINO ViT-B/8 72.2 / 46.9; MoCo ViT-B/16 71.9 / 44.0; DINO ViT-B/16 69.4 / 44.0; SSCD RN-50 67.6 / 36.0; VGG Gram (WikiArt only) 25.9. WikiArt Recall@10: CSD ViT-L 85.73 vs CLIP ViT-L 82.9. DINOv2 was not evaluated. — [Somepalli et al., Measuring Style Similarity in Diffusion Models (arXiv 2404.01292)](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01292v1)
- CSD is CLIP-initialised (ViT-B/ViT-L) and fine-tuned for 80k iterations on LAION-Styles (511,921 images, 3,840 style tags derived from LAION-Aesthetics 6+). It combines a multi-label contrastive loss with a self-supervised loss, uses augmentations that keep colour, texture and shape while removing content, drops photometric augmentations (colour jitter, blur), and compares images by cosine similarity. The authors note CLIP "is consistently biased towards image content". Code: [github.com/learn2phoenix/CSD](https://github.com/learn2phoenix/CSD). — [arXiv 2404.01292](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01292v1)
- Raw DINOv2 features show much lower style recall and much higher content recall than a style-disentangled method, which that paper attributes to the encoder's content-oriented bias. — [Contrastive-Augmented Flow Matching for Style-Content Disentanglement (arXiv 2607.12404)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12404)
- CLIP embeddings can cluster images by *overlaid text*, whereas DINOv2 clusters them by visual features. This matters for HUD-laden game frames. — [Towards an Explainable Comparison and Alignment of Feature Embeddings (arXiv 2506.06231)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06231)
- **Gram matrices (Gatys et al., CVPR 2016)**: style is represented by channel-correlation (Gram) matrices of VGG feature maps, G^l_ij = Σ_p f^l_ip f^l_jp. Secondary descriptions list ReLU1_1-ReLU5_1 as style layers and ReLU4_2 for content, and sources disagree on VGG-19 vs VGG-16. — [Gatys et al. 2016](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2016/gatys2016cvpr-image); [arXiv 1801.01933 (describing Gatys' layers)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.01933). Gram matrix size does not depend on input image size, so style loss can compare images of different resolutions. — [arXiv 2309.14859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14859)
- **DreamSim** (NeurIPS 2023) concatenates CLIP, OpenCLIP and DINO embeddings and fine-tunes on about 20k human similarity triplets generated by diffusion models. It targets mid-level similarity (layout, pose, semantics), whereas LPIPS compares small deep-feature patches and can rate images with similar look and feel but different content as close. A 2024 update added DINOv2 B/14 and SynCLR B/16 backbones. `pip install dreamsim`. — [DreamSim paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/9f09f316a3eaf59d9ced5ffaefe97e0f-Paper-Conference.pdf); [DreamSim GitHub](https://github.com/ssnl/dreamsim)
- **LAION aesthetic predictor**: a small MLP (768 → 1024 → 128 → 64 → 16 → 1) on OpenAI CLIP ViT-L/14 image embeddings, trained to predict 1-10 "how much do you like this image" ratings. The usual checkpoint is `sac+logos+ava1-l14-linearMSE.pth`. — [LAION-Aesthetics blog](https://laion.ai/blog/laion-aesthetics/); [pyiqa laion_aes arch docs](https://iqa-pytorch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/autoapi/pyiqa/archs/laion_aes_arch/index.html)
- **pyiqa** (IQA-PyTorch) bundles many full-reference and no-reference IQA metrics (for example MUSIQ, NIMA, TOPIQ including `topiq_iaa` for aesthetics) calibrated against official scripts, under an NTU S-Lab licence plus CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Check the licence before commercial use. — [pyiqa on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyiqa/)

### Inferences
- **Reliability for "asset render vs reference"**: the references are usually whole scenes with characters, sky and HUD, while the render is one prop on a neutral background, so content and framing differ far more than style. CLIP/DINO/DreamSim similarity will then be driven mostly by content and layout. CSD reduces this but is trained on artist styles (paintings, illustration), not 3D game shading styles, so its behaviour on game renders is unvalidated. Mitigations:
  1. compare like with like: crop reference regions that contain the same object class, and render the asset in a reference-like environment, camera and background;
  2. use *relative* tests: the render should be closer (cosine) to the target-style crops than to a negative set of off-style crops with similar content (for example the same prop type from photoreal or other-game references);
  3. use the embedding only as a tie-breaker beside the hand-crafted descriptors (sections 1-5), which are interpretable.
- **Gram matrices** are cheap to compute with VGG-16/19 and give an interpretable "texture statistics" distance, but they scored poorly on style retrieval (25.9 mAP@1 on WikiArt). Use them only for local texture/brush-stroke similarity between material crops, not for overall style.
- **Aesthetic scores** (LAION, NIMA, MUSIQ) measure general appeal or quality learned from photos and web images. They can flag "worse-looking" renders but say nothing about *matching* a style. Keep them out of the style gate or use them as an advisory only. The pyiqa licence is non-commercial.
- **Local cost (estimates, not measured)**: CLIP/CSD ViT-B on CPU is roughly a fraction of a second to about a second per 224² image; ViT-L is several times slower. With a modest GPU both run in milliseconds. Model downloads are about 0.3-1.7 GB. Embedding a few dozen references once and caching them is cheap, so CSD ViT-B (or CLIP ViT-B/16 if CSD weights are inconvenient) is the "worth it" optional component. Note that CLIP-family models see 224x224 crops, so fine detail and edge softness are invisible to them. Tile the image (for example 3x3 crops plus a whole-image view) and average the embeddings.
- DINOv2 is useful for *content* matching (finding reference crops of the same object type to compare against), not for style scoring.

### Gaps
- No study found that evaluates CSD, CLIP or DINOv2 on 3D game-render style matching (render vs in-game screenshot), and none evaluates DINOv2 on the CSD benchmark.
- Exact CPU latencies and parameter counts for CSD/CLIP/DreamSim on Windows were not found in the sources. The estimates above are unverified.
- Whether LPIPS/DreamSim distances correlate with artists' judgements of "same style, different content" was not found.

## 7. Existing tools and libraries (what to reuse)

### Takeaway
No single library produces an "art-style profile". The closest is the open-source Python Aesthetics Toolbox (Fourier slope, fractal dimension, PHOG, EOE, colour statistics, balance). Combine it with scikit-image/OpenCV/colour-science for colour and value work, Pylette or fast_colorthief for quick palettes, and optional torch models (CSD, MobileSAM, DreamSim) behind a flag.

### Cited Findings
- **Aesthetics Toolbox** (Redies group and collaborators): Python 3 ports of four groups' original MATLAB/Python 2 scripts, validated to match the originals except for HOG complexity and anisotropy when resizing is used, because MATLAB's resize differs from Pillow/scikit-image/OpenCV. It ships a streamlit GUI, a cloud version and a script-only version for batch and multithreaded use, and supports Windows, macOS and Linux. Measures: aspect ratio, RMS contrast, lightness entropy, HOG complexity, Gabor edge density, RGB/HSV/Lab channel stats, hue entropy, mirror symmetry/balance/DCM, CNN-based symmetry, three Fourier slope variants, 2-D/3-D fractal dimension, PHOG and CNN self-similarity, homogeneity, anisotropy, 1st/2nd-order EOE. It recommends at least 1024 px on the long side. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616); [GitHub: RBartho/Aesthetics-Toolbox](https://github.com/RBartho/Aesthetics-Toolbox)
- **Pylette**: k-means or median cut, CLI, alpha masking, MIT licence. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pylette/). **colorthief / fast_colorthief / modern_colorthief**: MMCQ palettes. fast_colorthief takes about 0.034 s vs 0.509 s for a 1200² JPEG. — [fast-colorthief PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/fast-colorthief/); [modern_colorthief benchmarks](https://modern-colorthief.readthedocs.io/en/latest/benchmarks.html)
- **scikit-image**: `measure.blur_effect` (above). **OpenCV contrib**: `cv2.saliency` (spectral residual, fine-grained). **colour-science**: `xy_to_CCT` with several methods (the default changed between versions, so pin the version). — [scikit-image](https://scikit-image.org/docs/stable/_sources/auto_examples/filters/plot_blur_effect.rst.txt); [OpenCV saliency](https://docs.opencv.org/4.5.4/dc/d0d/namespacecv_1_1saliency.html); [colour-science](https://colour.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/colour.xy_to_CCT.html)
- **pyaesthetics** (Python) implements Hasler-Süsstrunk colourfulness among other aesthetics measures. — [pyaesthetics docs](https://prettywebsite.readthedocs.io/en/stable/colorfulness.html)
- **Learned**: CSD ([GitHub](https://github.com/learn2phoenix/CSD)), DreamSim ([GitHub](https://github.com/ssnl/dreamsim)), pyiqa with LAION aesthetic/NIMA/MUSIQ (non-commercial licence; [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyiqa/)), MobileSAM ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14289v2); [Kornia](https://kornia.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models/mobile_sam.html)).

### Inferences
- **Dependency tiers**:
  - Tier 0: numpy + Pillow, enough for OKLab, palette k-means, histograms, FFT slope and the harmony fit. This tier extends the existing `tools/Style.py`.
  - Tier 1: + scikit-image + opencv-contrib-python, for multi-Otsu, segmentation, contours, saliency, blur_effect and estimate_sigma.
  - Tier 2 (optional): + colour-science (CCT) and the Aesthetics Toolbox scripts (EOE, PHOG, fractal dimension). Vendor only the needed functions, or call its script-only version, to avoid the streamlit GUI dependency.
  - Tier 3 (optional, torch): CSD ViT-B, MobileSAM, an intrinsic decomposition model.
- **Proposed style-profile JSON (one per reference set, plus per-image values and the spread across images)**:
  - `colour`: palette, hue families, harmony, chroma percentiles, M3 colourfulness, hue entropy, warm/cool, CCT of lit neutrals, shadow-light shift, chromatic contrast;
  - `value`: L percentiles, key, range, notan_3 and notan_5 (with shares, centres, fragmentation), RMS contrast, local/global contrast ratio, homogeneity;
  - `detail`: Fourier slope plus the orientation profile, HOG complexity, Gabor edge density, Canny fraction, blur_effect, edge-width histogram, noise sigma, within-material texture std, PHOG self-similarity, anisotropy, EOE;
  - `shape`: per-silhouette circularity, solidity and vertices, roundness ratio, rank-size slope;
  - `lighting`: key/fill stops, terminator softness, AO drop, rim share, specular density, haze slope and colour;
  - `learned` (optional): CSD centroid embedding plus the intra-set similarity distribution.

  Each numeric field should store median and IQR across the reference images, so a render can be judged against the *spread* of the references (for example pass if inside [p10, p90] of the reference distribution) rather than a single value. This fits the repo's "measured gates" rule.
- **Artist-facing output**: a one-page board (palette swatches, hue wheel with the fitted template, notan thumbnails, value histogram, edge map, slope number with a plain-language reading, silhouette overlays) is more useful than raw numbers. The repo already has a style-board convention (`tools/Board.py`, the `art-style` skill).

### Gaps
- I did not check licences of the Aesthetics Toolbox code (the paper is CC BY 4.0; the code licence was not confirmed), nor current Windows wheels for opencv-contrib and torch-based models.
- No existing open tool was found that outputs an art-direction-style profile (palette, notan, shape language) in one pass. This seems to be a gap the project would fill.

## 8. Robustness on perspective game frames (UI overlays, lighting, compression) and mitigation

### Takeaway
Most descriptors are sensitive to image size, resampler, colour space, compression artefacts, overlays and framing. Fix one analysis pipeline (size, resampler, colour space, implementation), mask HUD/UI and letterboxing, drop the highest spatial frequencies, measure per region and per lighting condition, and report distributions across many frames instead of single values.

### Cited Findings
- Values of many properties depend on image size, complexity measures most of all, while channel means and standard deviations are less affected. Use the same or similar resolution and at least 1024 px on the long side. Changing the aspect ratio also changes results. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- Resize functions in MATLAB, Pillow, scikit-image and OpenCV give different results, enough to break agreement for HOG complexity and anisotropy. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- Colour-model choice (for example sRGB vs Adobe RGB data) can strongly change computed colour properties even when the image looks the same. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- Fourier slope fitting excludes frequencies prone to artefacts (uneven illumination, rectangular sampling, raster screen, noise), and small implementation differences cause substantial deviations. — [Aesthetics Toolbox paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10616)
- Text in images (typeset or handwritten) is not scale-invariant in any direction, so text regions distort spectral statistics. — [Redies group results summarised via search; see Koch et al. 2010](https://doaj.org/article/3b3446d3895042209de57c074ce8b206)
- CLIP embeddings can group images by overlaid text. — [arXiv 2506.06231](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.06231)
- Edge-width blur metrics are sensitive to the threshold choice and to noise. — [arXiv 1810.08169](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.08169)
- The dark channel prior assumes non-sky patches contain dark pixels, and sky regions violate it. — [He et al.](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kaiming/publications/pami10dehaze.pdf)

### Inferences
Failure modes and mitigations (suggested):
- **HUD/UI overlays**: in video, HUD pixels barely change while the scene moves. Compute the per-pixel temporal std over 30 or more frames sampled across the clip and mask pixels with low std, then dilate. For single screenshots, mask high-contrast near-axis-aligned rectangles and text (an OCR or text detector such as EAST/PaddleOCR, or the user draws boxes once per game, since a HUD layout repeats). Also crop letterbox bars, watermarks and webcam overlays. UI biases the palette (pure white/black text, saturated icons), edge density, Fourier orientation peaks (0°/90°) and CLIP embeddings.
- **Perspective and framing**: sky and ground planes take large areas and dominate value and colour histograms, and distant objects shrink, so detail measures mix scales. Mitigate by (a) reporting statistics per region (sky / ground / props / characters via segmentation) as well as globally; (b) area-weighting by segment instead of pixel for palette "families"; (c) measuring shape metrics on near-orthographic views or concept-art turnarounds; (d) comparing renders against reference *crops* at matched object scale (object height about the same fraction of the crop).
- **Lighting and time of day**: a game's palette and value key vary by scene (day, night, interior, weather). Cluster frames by global stats (mean L, b, CCT) and report one profile per lighting condition, or the user labels them. Separate "material colour" (lit, low-shadow pixels of each segment, or the intrinsic albedo) from "scene lighting" so an asset's texture colours are matched to albedo, not to lit and tone-mapped colour.
- **Post-processing**: bloom, vignette, colour grading, depth of field and motion blur alter value range, edge softness and slope. Crop the central about 80% to avoid vignette, discard frames with heavy motion blur (blur_effect above a set threshold, or low Laplacian variance relative to the clip median), and treat DoF-blurred backgrounds as separate regions. Note that grading is part of the style: record it (for example shadow and highlight tint) rather than remove it, but the 3D asset pipeline controls it in the engine's post stack, not in textures.
- **Compression (JPEG, YouTube/H.264)**: 8x8/macroblock blocking adds energy at specific high frequencies and grid orientations, chroma subsampling (4:2:0) blurs colour edges at half resolution, and banding creates false edges in gradients. Mitigate by using the highest-bitrate source, sampling I-frames, downscaling to the analysis size (which averages out blocking), fitting the Fourier slope only up to the Redies cut-off (256 cycles at 1024²), measuring colour edges on L only, and estimating noise only on flat regions.
- **Concept art vs screenshots**: concept art is not rendered by the engine (painted edges, no HUD, often no tone mapping). Keep separate profiles for "concept" and "in-game" references and decide per descriptor which one the asset must match. Usually colours, shapes and value design come from concept art, while edge softness, texture density and lighting come from in-game frames.
- **Statistics, not single values**: compute descriptors on 20-50 frames and store the median and IQR. Gate renders against reference percentiles. Check gate stability by leave-one-out: every reference frame should pass the gate built from the others.

### Gaps
- I found no published study quantifying how much HUD overlays, YouTube compression or tone mapping shift these specific descriptors. The mitigations above are engineering judgement and should be validated on the project's own references (for example by measuring descriptor change between a clean screenshot and the same frame after re-encoding).
- The "text is not scale-invariant" finding came from a search summary of Redies-group work. I did not open the specific paper, so the exact citation is unverified.
